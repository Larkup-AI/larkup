The Video Intelligence marketplace tool lets you investigate a local file, a YouTube video, or an entire playlist in Chat. It creates seekable source evidence rather than relying on a generated summary, then uses a bounded rewind when a complex answer needs stronger proof.

## Installing the tool

Go to **Settings → Marketplace** and install **Video Intelligence**. You only need to do this once.

  <img src="/images/new/marketplace.png" alt="Marketplace showing Video and Audio tool" />
</Frame>

Once installed, the **Media** tab appears in the Data page.

## Importing a video

Open **Data → Media**. You can either upload a file from your computer or paste a YouTube URL (single video or playlist, up to 10 URLs at once).

<Frame caption="Importing a YouTube video from the Media tab">
  <img src="/images/new/upload-video-data-page.png" alt="Media tab with YouTube URL import" />
</Frame>

Before the import starts, Larkup shows you the detected duration, expected processing time, and estimated API cost. Click **Import** to confirm.

Indexing runs in the background and covers:

- Speech transcription with timestamps
- Adaptive attention: cheap coverage and activity cues, then focused visual analysis
- OCR on visible text
- A full video summary plus independent timestamped evidence sections

When a source exposes a usable timestamped transcript—for example YouTube
manual or automatic captions—Larkup fetches and indexes the complete caption
timeline directly. This is independent from frame analysis and avoids a paid
audio-transcription pass. Audio transcription is used only when the source does
not provide usable timed captions.

For supported Video Knowledge Engine revisions, Larkup keeps source evidence
separate from search projections. A detailed answer is grounded in a seekable
transcript, OCR, or visual-evidence range rather than a generated summary.
Visual anchors are not presented as continuous observation: when the evidence
cannot establish a detail, the agent can reopen a bounded raw-source range
instead of guessing.

For every video question, Chat starts a fresh investigation through a temporal
tree: **chapters → scenes → events and states → source evidence**. Simple
questions normally resolve from the relevant timeframe summary and its attached
evidence. For a complex question, Chat selects the best candidate ranges, then
uses bounded tracking, OCR, comparison, counting, or visual inspection only
where needed. The timeframe tree is cached briefly per video revision for fast
follow-ups, but evidence retrieval still runs on every question.

You can watch download, transcription, visual analysis, and embedding progress in real time in the Media panel.

Before importing video, choose a vision-capable model in **Settings → AI Models → Vision Model**. This setting is independent from the chat model, so a workspace can use a specialized VLM for frame analysis while using a different model for conversation.

## Asking questions

Switch to **Chat**. Ask who said something, what happened at a specific point, or what the outcome was. Larkup retrieves the matching timestamped segments and the answer includes an embedded player positioned at the cited moment.

## Evidence and limits

Larkup can answer facts that are visible, audible, or reliably derivable from
the authorized video. It does not identify people, infer facts absent from the
source, or present an approximate timestamp as exact. When the indexed evidence
is insufficient for a visual detail, Larkup can request a bounded source
inspection; large scans are kept within configured duration, frame, and spend
budgets and may require background refinement instead of an unbounded chat run.

For people-counting requests, Larkup reports bounded anonymous tracks (for
example, `Person A — 00:10–00:25`) with source coverage and occlusion limits.
It does not identify people, perform facial recognition, or match a person
between videos.

For a high-resolution OCR, code, visual comparison, or count request, Larkup
first reserves the request’s pessimistic budget. If the requested coverage is
larger than the configured cap, it creates a refinement request with an expiry
time instead of silently scanning the full video. Approving that request is a
workspace-scoped, spend-affecting action; declining or expiry leaves the
existing video knowledge available for later questions.

OCR and visual inspection outputs are stored as additional evidence revisions.
They retain their source ranges and provider provenance and are labelled
**uncalibrated analysis** until validated against the evaluation corpus. They
never overwrite the original transcript or frames.

## Rollout and rollback

The **Video Knowledge Engine** setting in **Settings → Marketplace Tools →
Video Intelligence** controls the evidence-first pipeline. It is enabled by default.
If a workspace needs a rollback, disable it before starting new processing: the
existing compatible media pipeline remains available and already-active Video
Knowledge revisions remain readable. Re-enable it to create a new revision on
the next processing run; it never mutates a prior revision in place.

## Retention and deletion

Deleting a Media asset removes its stored source, searchable documents, vector
entries, and all associated Video Knowledge revisions, evidence, jobs, and
pending refinements within that workspace. Frame derivatives used for a bounded
inspection are temporary worker artifacts and are removed when the inspection
finishes or fails.

<Tip>
  Need to index a video again after changing your transcription provider or language setting? Go to
  **Data → Media → View Uploads** and select the indexing action for that asset.
</Tip>
